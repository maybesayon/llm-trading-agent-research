# 8. Limitations

The limitations below are boundaries on the conclusions this study can support, not defects discovered after the fact; each was accepted as a design trade-off and is paired with the section that manages it.

**Data.** The news corpus samples four financial websites (Dong et al., 2024) and therefore represents one slice of the information environment; strategies are evaluated relative to this information set, not to all news that existed (§3.1). Point-in-time correctness depends on source timestamp accuracy, which we test but cannot fully guarantee; where granularity is coarse, the conservative execution convention of §3.2 protects integrity at the cost of signal freshness. Track B relies on free news APIs whose firm-level coverage and precision are verification items, and its six-month span limits it to directional validation. Residual contamination risk remains even under the three-condition protocol: anonymization can leave stylistic or contextual cues that identify entities, and no procedure can prove that memorized knowledge exerted no influence — which is precisely why Track A and Track B are designed to check one another (§3.4, §5.6).

**Modeling.** All LLM results are conditional on a single pinned open-weight model and on the specific frozen prompts of Appendix D; we do not explore prompt variations or model families, so conclusions attach to this instrument, not to "LLMs" as a class (§4.7). Stochastic decoding is bounded by the three-seed dispersion analysis rather than eliminated. Most fundamentally, the rationale audit measures consistency between stated reasons and behavior; it cannot establish that a rationale describes the causal mechanism of a decision (§6.1), and no claim in this paper depends on such a reading.

**Evaluation.** A backtest is a simulation: it omits market impact, execution latency, capacity constraints, and the feedback effects of actual participation, and no result here guarantees or predicts live performance. The primary window spans two years in a single market and the most liquid segment of it — a deliberately conservative testbed (§3.1) that limits generalization in the opposite direction as well: a null here does not preclude value in less efficient segments. Statistical power against small effects is limited, intervals will be wide, and the regime analysis is descriptive by design (§4.5, §4.6); financial inference from a single window is inherently uncertain, which the pre-committed interpretation rules of §5.10 and §7.1 are constructed to respect rather than obscure.

**Audit.** Human validation of rationale classification rests on a single annotator with a released subsample as the remedy (§6.2); perturbation quality is spot-checked on a fixed 10% fraction rather than exhaustively; polarity flips necessarily alter some content, so sentiment sensitivity is bounded rather than isolated (§6.4); and the audited sample is budget-constrained (§6.6). Accordingly, audit findings characterize one agent under one model in one setting, and their proper interpretive weight is fixed in §7.3.

# 9. Future Work

The extensions below follow from the limitations; we distinguish those that are direct, realistic continuations from those that are more speculative. Realistic near-term extensions: repeating the protocol on mid- and small-capitalization universes, which are plausibly less efficient than the deliberately conservative mega-cap testbed used here; extending the horizon toward multi-decade evaluation in the manner of Li et al. (2025); running the frozen protocol across several model families to test instrument dependence; full-fidelity replication of named agent systems, which our simplified reference agent deliberately does not attempt; multi-annotator validation of the rationale taxonomy; and a longer forward (paper-trading) window, which strengthens Track B's role at the cost of calendar time. More speculative directions, contingent on tooling and access: white-box causal analysis of agent decisions via activation-level intervention, which could convert the audit's consistency findings into mechanism findings; extension to other asset classes such as cryptocurrencies or fixed income, where information environments differ qualitatively; and study of market-level effects if LLM-driven strategies become widespread — a question this single-agent design cannot touch. Any real-world deployment consideration would additionally require the market-impact, capacity, and latency modeling that backtesting omits, and none of the present study's outputs should be read as deployment guidance.

# 10. Conclusion (Results-Pending Framework)

*Status note: to be rewritten in declarative form once experiments conclude; the commitments below bound that rewriting.*

This study addresses a contradiction at the center of the LLM-trading literature: developer-reported backtests that beat the market, and independent re-evaluation under which those advantages disappear. Its response is methodological: a pre-specified, bias-controlled evaluation framework — a six-strategy ladder on identical data, a three-condition memorization-quantification protocol, an out-of-cutoff validation track, corrected performance statistics, and a rationale audit that tests whether agents' explanations track their behavior. The framework is designed to determine where, if anywhere, along the path from price signal to autonomous agent value is added, how much of apparent historical performance memorization explains, and whether stated rationales can be trusted as summaries of agent behavior. The paper makes no claim that any strategy outperforms any other, and no hypothesis is validated by anything reported here; every such conclusion depends on the experiments specified in Sections 3–6 and will be stated, when available, under the interpretation rules fixed in Sections 5 and 7. If the eventual results survive the framework's controls, their significance will lie in being the first such evidence produced under conditions designed to exclude the known artifacts; if they do not, the significance will lie in showing that a purpose-built, low-cost protocol suffices to separate evaluation artifact from genuine capability — and, in either case, in the released code, cached model outputs, and frozen protocol that allow others to check both.
