# 6. Rationale Audit: Pre-Specified Framework

*Status note: as with Section 5, no experiments have been conducted. This section specifies the audit's constructs, procedures, metrics, and interpretation rules in advance; all tables are placeholders. The audit's design choices, alternatives considered, and remaining limitations are stated explicitly, because the value of an audit depends on the auditor's methods being open to the same scrutiny as the audited system.*

## 6.1 Constructs and Design Rationale

The audit distinguishes two properties of an explanation that are easily conflated: *plausibility*, whether an explanation is convincing to a human reader, and *faithfulness*, whether it reflects the actual basis of the model's decision (Jacovi & Goldberg, 2020). Trading-agent rationales are, by construction, plausible — they are fluent analyses citing recognizable drivers — but plausibility is precisely what Turpin et al. (2023) show can coexist with systematic misrepresentation of a model's true decision basis. The audit therefore measures behavioral consistency: whether decisions change under controlled input perturbations in the manner the stated rationales imply. Following Jacovi and Goldberg's (2020) argument that binary faithfulness is an unrealistic bar, all audit metrics are graded proportions rather than pass/fail judgments.

Three alternative audit designs were considered and rejected. Attribution methods (gradients, attention) were rejected because their own faithfulness is contested and because they attribute token-level influence rather than decision-level drivers, which is the granularity at which trading rationales speak. Directly querying the model about its reasoning was rejected because self-reports are the very object under suspicion (Turpin et al., 2023). Probing internal activations was rejected as beyond scope: it requires white-box instrumentation whose findings are difficult to map onto the trading action space, though we note it as a complementary avenue for future work. Input-perturbation testing was chosen because it is model-agnostic, operates at the decision level, and produces directly interpretable quantities. Its principal limitation is stated at the outset: perturbations establish consistency between words and behavior, not ground-truth causation — a rationale could be consistent with behavior yet still not describe the mechanism that produced it. The audit's conclusions are accordingly framed as consistency findings.

## 6.2 Rationale Taxonomy and Classification

Every S6 rationale is logged (§4.1). Each is classified into a taxonomy frozen before experiments: *sentiment-driven*, *price/momentum-driven*, *risk-management-driven*, or *mixed/other*. Classification is performed by the pinned LLM under a fixed prompt, with a random, seeded subsample of [N, specified in Appendix D] rationales independently labeled by the author; agreement between machine and human labels will be reported as a continuous Cohen's kappa with a bootstrap confidence interval, not as a pass/fail verdict. The pre-specified value κ = 0.70 serves as an interpretive guideline rather than a statistical truth boundary: if the kappa point estimate falls below it, all analyses will use the human labels on the subsample only, with the reduced scope reported, and the kappa value itself will be reported in either case. We acknowledge that validation against a single annotator is a limitation; the labeled subsample will be released so that others can re-annotate and re-derive the agreement figure. **Table 6: Rationale-type prevalence by regime (to be populated after experiments)** reports the taxonomy distribution overall and within the regime partitions of §4.6, with observation counts.

## 6.3 Robustness Test: Neutral Paraphrase

For a pre-specified, seeded random sample of decision days, each news input is paraphrased to preserve content and sentiment while varying surface form; paraphrases are generated by the pinned model under a fixed prompt, and a fixed 10% of audited decisions — selected by seeded sampling stratified across regimes — undergo manual spot checks for content preservation; the percentage is frozen before evaluation as a protocol parameter (Appendix D). The decision is then regenerated under identical conditions. The reported metric is the *decision flip rate* under paraphrase. Interpretation rule, fixed in advance: because a paraphrase changes no decision-relevant information, flips indicate sensitivity to surface form; the flip rate will be reported with a bootstrap interval and compared against the across-seed flip rate from §4.7, which provides the baseline level of decision instability attributable to sampling nondeterminism alone. A paraphrase flip rate indistinguishable from the seed-only baseline would indicate robustness to wording; an elevated rate would not, and would qualify all rationale-level findings.

## 6.4 Sensitivity Test: Polarity Flip

For the same sampled days, a second variant reverses the sentiment polarity of the news while holding non-sentiment content as fixed as practicable (entities, figures, and event descriptions retained; evaluative language inverted). The reported metric is the *directional agreement rate*: the proportion of decisions that change in the direction a sentiment-driven reading implies (long toward flat under negative flip, and conversely). The metric is computed only over decision-days where a response in the implied direction is observable — a flat position cannot respond to a negative flip, nor a long position to a positive one — and the count of excluded, unobservable cases is reported alongside, since silently including them would inflate apparent consistency. The residual confound is acknowledged rather than argued away: inverting evaluative language necessarily alters some content, so polarity flips bound rather than isolate sentiment sensitivity. Construction quality is checked by the same spot-checking procedure as §6.3.

## 6.5 Rationale–Behavior Consistency

The audit's central quantity links §6.2 to §6.4: among decisions whose logged rationale is classified sentiment-driven, the proportion that respond to polarity flips; among decisions classified price/momentum-driven, the proportion that do not. **Table 7: Consistency between stated driver and behavioral response (to be populated after experiments)** reports these proportions with intervals, overall and by regime — the regime breakdown being descriptive under the same rules as §4.6 and §5.7. Interpretation rules, fixed in advance: high consistency supports treating this agent's rationales as informative summaries of its behavior *in this setting*; low consistency indicates the rationales should not be relied upon as explanations, which would itself bear on the interpretability claims made for agent architectures (Yu et al., 2023). Neither outcome licenses conclusions about LLM faithfulness in general, about other architectures, or about the mechanism underlying any consistency observed.

## 6.6 Pre-Specified Scope and Exploratory Extensions

The audit sample size is constrained by inference cost: perturbation tests multiply LLM calls per decision day. The sample size will be determined after the pilot study based on measured inference cost, but the selection rule is fixed in advance: the audit uses the largest regime-stratified, seeded random sample that fits within the pre-specified audit budget while maintaining representation across market conditions. The exact budget and the resulting sample size are recorded in Appendix D as protocol parameters — set by the rule and the pilot's cost measurement, not by any examination of results. All analyses in §6.2–§6.5 are pre-specified; any additional analysis suggested by the data — for example, examining rationale length or specific entity mentions — will be conducted only after the pre-specified results are fixed, and reported in a separate subsection explicitly labeled exploratory, consistent with the frozen-protocol principle of Section 5.
